# Customer Reliability Engineering Roles

## Purpose

This page describes the individual contributor career path for Customer Reliability Engineers (CREs) at Octopus. It's here to help us:

- Understand what's expected at each level
- Understand what's needed to progress
- Support each other's growth as the CRE function grows

If you haven't read the [Customer Reliability Engineer role introduction](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Cfy07S670iBTjiupIpLmZB6G2Aw1Sr49/view), start there. It explains the problem CRE solves and why it's shaped the way it is.

## What CRE is

CRE is a support engineering function that builds the machinery that makes proactive support possible. Support engineers work the queue. CREs improve the processes and systems that make Support more efficient, so more problems get caught before they become tickets and the ones that do arrive are faster to solve.

CRE has four focus areas:

- **Internal AI tooling for the support team**: tooling that gives support engineers better context, smarter investigations and less mechanical work
- **Cloud instance monitoring and health**: watching for problems in customer instances and getting alerts to the right people before a customer notices
- **Customer health data for account teams**: data built around the Adoption Index that Adoption Pods, CE’s and CA’s can use in the conversations they're having
- **Antipattern detection and proactive outreach**: finding the configurations that reliably lead to tickets, and turning them into a signal someone can act on

## What CRE isn't

CREs aren't part of the support queue. You won't be rostered on tickets, and you aren't a second line of escalation for customer issues. When you come across a customer problem in your work, please route it through our normal support channels so it's handled like any other ticket.

CRE also isn't a way to add headcount to the queue in disguise. The whole point is to stop things reaching the queue at all.

## How we work in CRE

We share the principles in [How We Work in Support](../Support/README.md#how-we-work-in-support). Customer impact comes first, support is a team sport, and initiative matters at every level. On top of those, a few things are specific to CRE:

**We build for the people who use it.** Our customers are support engineers, Adoption Pods and account teams. If they don't use what we build, it doesn't matter how good it is.

**Knowledge is the foundation.** Our AI tooling is only as good as the knowledge underneath it. Fixing the KB is AI work, not a preamble to it.

**AI != automated responses.** The real value is in connecting systems, so an engineer has the context they need without switching between five windows.

**AI isn't fire and forget.** Everything we ship needs evaluation, feeding and maintenance to stay useful, and that's our job, not a side hustle.

**We taste the food.** Our own agents run on the governance we sell to customers: scoped access, service accounts and audit logs that attribute activity back to a human.

**Noise destroys trust.** An alert or signal nobody trusts is worse than no signal at all.

## Your career journey in CRE

CRE is a technical progression path within Support. Some engineers love the direct customer work and want to become exceptionally good at it, and we need those people. Others are drawn to building tools, automation and AI. CRE gives that second group somewhere to grow without leaving Support.

Levels L1 to L3 focus on **individual mastery**: learning Octopus, our data and our systems deeply, and owning more of what we build.

Levels L4 and L5 mark a shift from **self-improvement** to **organisational impact**. At this stage, getting better at the craft on your own shows diminishing returns. The path forward is multiplying your impact through other people, other teams and the decisions Octopus makes about its customers.

Each PD includes a planning horizon, impact radius and the level of direction needed, borrowed from our SRE ladder, to make the step between levels easier to see.

Tip: open adjacent levels side by side and compare them. It's the fastest way to see what's needed to progress, and a good starting point for a conversation with your manager.
