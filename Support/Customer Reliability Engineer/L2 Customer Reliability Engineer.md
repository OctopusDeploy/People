# L2: Customer Reliability Engineer

> I'm a reliable, curious and self-sufficient reliability engineer. I own well-defined pieces of our monitoring, health data, antipattern detection and AI tooling from start to finish, and I know when to ask for help. What I build is used by support engineers and account teams every day, and I care about whether it actually helps them.

**Planning horizon:** Current and next cycle
**Impact radius:** Self and some teammates, plus the people who use what I build
**Responsibility and direction needed:** Works independently on well-defined work, with weekly check-ins

## Impact

- I deliver features and improvements across our CRE systems independently
- I work directly with support engineers, CE’s and CA’s to understand what they need, and to check that what I build is useful
- I help teammates level up through pairing and code review
- I keep the systems we own healthy, and I take my share of looking after them when they break

## I build and run reliable tooling independently

- I have solid working knowledge of Octopus across its core surfaces, and I build tooling that correctly interprets what it sees in a customer instance
- I build and improve features across our four focus areas: cloud instance monitoring, customer health data, antipattern detection and internal AI tooling for the support team
- I build AI tooling that connects systems together, like pulling instance logs, deployment history and worker health into a ticket, so a support engineer can start troubleshooting without switching between five windows
- I write evaluations for the agents and prompts I work on, and I can show whether a change made them better or worse
- I treat our knowledge base as the foundation for our AI work, and I flag gaps and stale content when I find them
- I reduce noise in our alerts and signals, because an alert nobody trusts is worse than no alert at all
- I write code that's clean, tested and documented well enough that a teammate can maintain it without me

## I turn customer signal into something people can act on

- I build and maintain the dashboards and data that account teams use, built around the Adoption Index rather than a score of our own
- I make sure what I produce is accurate, current and useful in a QBR or a pod stand-up, not just a raw query result
- I build and tune antipattern detection so that when a signal fires, it's worth someone's time to reach out
- I document what each signal means and what someone should do when they see it
- I spot patterns and edge cases across customers and share them with the team and with support engineers

## I strengthen the team and how we work

- I recognise problems early and get in and fix them, regardless of whose fault they are
- I give helpful feedback in reviews and pairing, and I take feedback well
- I flag gaps in our processes and suggest improvements
- I hold myself accountable and follow through on my commitments
- I keep the people who depend on my work informed, especially when plans change

## What success looks like

At this level, success means delivering reliable tooling independently and making sure it gets used. These examples reflect the principles in [How We Work in Support](../Support/README.md#how-we-work-in-support).

- Own a piece of CRE tooling from idea to production with light guidance
- Show that something you built made support engineers or account teams faster or better informed
- Keep false positives and alert noise trending down on the signals you own
- Write evaluations and documentation as part of the work
- Help teammates through pairing, reviews or knowledge sharing
- Raise patterns you see across customers with the right people
